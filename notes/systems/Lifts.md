# Lifts

Lift types run from a fixed single and double through the fixed triple, fixed quad, high-speed quad, six-pack and eight-pack, gondola, funitel, 3S gondola and aerial tram, plus a heli-ski "lift" with no cable. Type sets seats per chair and cable speed. A lift can be upgraded to a better type on the same line for the price difference. Heli is the only lift that charges per ride; everything else is covered by [[Tickets]].

[[GOAP]] plans every ride: walk or ski to the base, join the line, ride to the top. Lines drain [[Patience]]. With no line attendant a line is a loose crowd fanning out from the loading spot, where arrivals take the nearest place on its edge, everyone shuffles up as chairs load, and now and then someone slips ahead; with one it's a rope corral: a short run a chair wide to the load, fed by two straight lanes a chair wide that take turns, sized to the line ([[Lift Lines]]). A guest won't join a line with a wait over 30 clock minutes (90 before their first ride of the day: they came to ski), and if every lift they can use is over that cap they leave. A lift whose base has under 30 cm of [[Snow]] goes on hold, and runs again from 35 cm ([[Snowmaking]] is how to get it there early). Total lift capacity sets how crowded [[Demand]] thinks the resort is.

Every cable lift has two operators, one in each station's hut. The player can add a line attendant ($750 a day each, like the operators) in the lift popup. Without one, strangers rarely share a chair: the next group in line joins only if they all fit, and only 90% of the time for each further group. So with a line behind them, unattended quads leave the base about three quarters full. An attendant fills every seat, splitting groups and calling singles. Line waits count how full chairs leave, and the popup shows it. Staff stand at their posts while the lift is open. Sweepers wait for [[Packed Snow]].

The [[Trails]] off a lift's top decide which skill levels it serves.

At the top, riders stand up from their seats and glide about 4 m straight off the ramp onto the station's flat apron, away from the cable. Then they peel left or right by where they sat (outer seats harder), and only then ski toward their next target. Around each station's apron the earthwork cuts and fills only ground steeper than 15° from its edge, and doesn't make natural slopes steeper ([[Terrain]]). Some riders fall getting off: about 3% of beginners on fixed-grip chairs and 1% on detachables, fewer for better skiers, none on gondolas.

Not built yet, from [[Vision]] and [[Next Steps]]: wear, breakdowns, wind holds, downloading, and refusing overlapping lifts ([[Lift Operations]]); staff who commute rather than just standing at their posts; sweepers ([[Packed Snow]]); and lift lines that wrap around buildings.

Stations (2026-10-13). Each end has a terminal round its bullwheel, the same shape at both ends, and riders get on and off under it at a load point in front of the wheel on the riders' cable, marked by a red line. The chair loop runs up one cable, half way round the top wheel, down the other and round the base wheel, so a chair comes round behind the riders. Stations are about as wide as their chairs: a housing on four round posts just outside the widest chair's sweep, with the cable, rail and bullwheel inside it and its underside at 2.6 m, about 0.9 m over a standing skier, the chairs hanging out of slots along each cable (`models-src/lib/station_kit.py`); and the line's rows are a seat (0.6 m) a guest, as wide as the chair they board, so they stand between the posts. A fixed-grip lift (double, triple, fixed quad) has a short station (`lift_station.py`): the vault from behind the wheel to over the load point 3 m in front of it. A detachable (high-speed quad, gondola; the 6-pack has a wider copy, `lift_terminal_wide.py`) has a long terminal (`lift_terminal.py`), modelled on Red Dog's at Palisades: a 14 m housing on four posts with the bullwheel inside it at the back; the chairs, off the haul rope, run flat at loading height round under the wheel and along a rail, load 4.5 m in front of it, and roll on through the front of the terminal, slowed to 1 m/s the whole way and speeding up over 6 m as they leave, so they bunch inside as real ones do. The cable stays at terminal height through the terminal, then runs on its profile (Towers and spans, below). Lines, corrals, crowds and the attendant's post measure from the load point; the apron reaches forward under the terminal, and a save whose station ground isn't level under the whole terminal (levelled before the terminals got longer) is re-levelled when it loads (`levelStationAprons`); posts and huts run a metre into the ground; a lift must be long enough for both terminals (22 m fixed-grip, 36 m detachable). Throughput and the planner's ride time come from the time round the loop (`LoopTime`, `RideTime`). Code: `internal/world/lift_station.go`.

Station houses (2026-10-14): the gondola and the tram have their own terminals (`gondola_terminal.py`, `tram_terminal.py`, built on `station_kit.add_station_house`): the cabins dock on a platform over an enclosed ground floor (2.2 m up for the gondola, 3 m for the tram), so on sloping ground they stay clear of the snow, under a hall roof on six columns. The line files in single file (no crowd or corral) through a lobby in the middle of the back wall (`StationGeom.DoorHalf`); riders board up out of sight, and riders getting off walk in to the door's span, straight back out of it, and turn for their run once clear of the back wall (`walkOutUnloading`). The walls are steering points (`wallPoints`); the operator works inside. The gondola's cabins are 2.1 m square, eight riders seated on two facing benches.

Aerial tram (2026-10-14): two 100-rider cabins (Jackson Hole's carry 100, Palisades' 110; 8.6 × 3.5 m, sized from Portland's Gangloff cabins, 7.62 × 3.35 m for 78 and an operator, so about 0.27 m² a standing rider inside against Portland's 0.29), each on its own track 3.2 m off the line (two track ropes 0.8 m apart, so four in all), shuttling between the terminals: one goes up as the other comes down, they dock at the same time, wait 45 s and swap. It runs on the chair loop: two places half a loop apart, the loop a round trip (up, docked, down, docked), so progress 0 is a cabin leaving the base (the whole line crowds in, attendant or not) and the unload point one arriving at the top; only where a cabin is differs (`tramAt`: it never turns and keeps to its track). Riders board and leave from the platform between the docks, on the line. The track runs 7 m over the platform through the terminal (the drive house hung in the roof's back end), then on its profile over lattice towers (`tram_tower.py`), spans of up to 1.5 km. At least 300 m long; 10 m/s; $7M for the terminals and $2,000 a metre. Not in the demo. Code: `internal/world/tram.go`.

More lifts (2026-10-14). The single (one rider, Mad River Glen's; fixed-grip station). The high-speed 8-pack (`chair_8pack.py`), its cables 2.6 m off the line (`WideCableGap`) so its 4.8 m chairs pass clear, on its own wider terminal and towers (`lift_terminal_8pack.py`, `tower_wide.py`); a 6-pack upgrades to it. Bubble covers, bought in a 6-pack's or 8-pack's popup ($6,000 a chair, `Lift.Bubble`, saved): a tinted hood down over the riders on the line and up in the terminals (`chair_kit.bubble`), drawn see-through after everything solid so the riders show (the hood is its own model, `chair_*_hood*.py`, faded by `dynamic.frag`'s `uFade`; it casts no shadow), and a better ride (quality 0.65 against 0.5). The funitel (`funitel_cabin.py`): 24 riders (twelve seated, twelve standing; Péclet's carry 24, 19 seated) in wide cabins hung from both of a way's two haul ropes, 1.6 m either side of its track 3.6 m off the line (Péclet's ways are 7.2 m apart, its ropes about 3 m), on its own towers (`funitel_tower.py`); steady in wind, for wind holds when they come. The 3S gondola (`tri_cabin.py`): 28 riders, all seated, as Zermatt's Matterhorn Glacier Ride (Peak 2 Peak's carry 28, 24 seated; Leitner's Symphony seats 28 with room for 7 standing), in a cabin 3.85 m square with 2.5 m inside, as 3S cabins are (a floor about 3.7 m square), a carriage on two track ropes gripped to the haul rope between them, run like the tram between its terminals, on the tram's towers. Both dock in station houses (`station_kit.add_cabin_house`, `world.cabinHouse`), as the gondola does. Cabin riders ride without skis (they go in the racks outside), seated or standing as their slot says (`MeshSlot.Pose`, `Back` for facing the back; `Guest.LiftSeat`); the tram's riders stand. Riders are 0.48 m apart shoulder to shoulder. Every loop now turns round bullwheels `CableOffset` in radius, and a cable is drawn as its `RopeOffsets`. Code: `internal/world/cabin_lifts.go`.

Towers and spans (2026-10-14). An aerial lift's towers are placed from the ground along its line, not evenly spaced (`internal/world/lift_profile.go`). From each support, greedily up the line, the next tower goes where it costs least per metre of line gained (a fixed cost per tower plus its height), as short as keeps the span clear of the ground (terminal height plus a margin over the bare ground, the highest across the cables, easing down to terminal height near each terminal). So towers bunch on knolls and breaks of slope, and a hollow is crossed in one high span. Each type has its own limits (`ropewaySpec`): chairs 8–24 m towers and spans up to 85 m (detachables 9–26 m and 100 m, the gondola to 30 m and 140 m), the first tower within 40–50 m of a terminal; the funitel 12–45 m and 400 m; the tram and 3S 20–80 m and 1.5 km. Between supports the cable runs straight with a parabolic sag (2% of the span at its middle for chairs, 3% for the tram). Its ends meet each terminal's cable over the snow as it is now; tower tops are fixed when the profile is laid out (on first use, again after an apron is carved). Chairs ride it (`CableYAt`, from the distance along the line), and the cable mesh bends at each tower. Each tower model is stretched to its tower's height, to the metre, in the renderer (`render/tower_heights.go`): the head moves whole, the shaft stretches and spreads wider at the foot. The T-bar keeps evenly spaced towers and a cable 4.5 m over the snow. On Boreal's Goals Test the three chairs come out with 4–9 towers each, 8–18 m tall; as a tram, Lift1's 770 m line has a single 59 m tower.

Getting to the line (2026-10-14). Guests head for where they'd join the line, re-read every tick so a growing line draws them on. Within 20 m of it (or by the station when it's in the way) a walker drops their route, whose cells end where the back was when it was laid and run straight through the station, and steers there directly; the route comes back only past 30 m, so the two don't take turns. When the station is in the way, everyone (walkers and skiers) goes round its outside: the near front corner, the back corner, then in from behind, a station house only by its door (`Lift.StationDetour`, `sim.lineApproach`). From outside a station house's walls, being near the load point no longer counts as at the line.

Code: `internal/world/lift.go`. Upgrades: `UpgradeLift` in `internal/world/world.go`.
