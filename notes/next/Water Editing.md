# Water Editing

Creeks and lakes as things the [[Scenario Editor]] can fix by hand, the way it edits [[Trails]]: an import still finds them from OpenStreetMap and the lidar, but what it finds is stored as world-space lines and outlines, and those are what the ground is built from. A creek that misses its channel or breaks at a junction, or a lake outline that's off, is dragged into place instead of worked around in the fitting code. Follows [[Creeks and Lakes]] (the fitting: `geo.RefineStreams`, `geo.WaterMask`); part of [[Terrain Import]].

## Decisions

From the user, 2026-10-13:

- **The editor's copies are the truth.** An import fits OpenStreetMap to the lidar once and stores the result in the save as world-space creek lines and lake outlines; the Creeks and Lakes layers build from those. OpenStreetMap only seeds a new import, and the fitting never moves a hand-placed point.
- **The ground follows on release.** While a node is dragged only the line moves; when the drag ends, or a node is added or deleted, the creek's channel is re-carved or the lake re-levelled around it, and the water, ice, snow and trees there redrawn.
- **First version: edit what's there.** Drag, insert and delete nodes on creek lines and lake outlines, and delete a whole creek or lake. Drawing new ones, per-creek controls over where it's open, and a re-fit button come later.

## How it fits

- **Where the water lives.** A **Water** terrain layer, between Erode and Creeks in the Layers panel, fits the import's OpenStreetMap water to the ground and keeps the result in the world (`World.Water`: `Creeks`, `Lakes`, `Fitted`), saved as its own section of the scenario, apart from the import (`TerrainBase` keeps OpenStreetMap's water as fetched). The layer stack carries the water (`geo.NewLayerStack(base, water)`); the Creeks and Lakes layers carve and level it and never read OpenStreetMap. The Water row shows what it holds and has a refit button (after asking) that fits it from OpenStreetMap again, losing hand edits. The OpenStreetMap overlay draws OpenStreetMap's own lines, so the two can be compared.
- **Seeding at import.** Creeks: `TraceStreams` then `RefineStreams`, as now, then ends that met in OpenStreetMap are joined again (the gap the user found: each line's end moved across its own channel separately). Lakes: `WaterMask`'s fitted water traced into an outline (marching squares on the 1.25 m lattice) and simplified to about a metre. From then on the Lakes layer levels exactly what's inside the outline, at its level (the low percentile of the ground inside, as now).
- **Local rebuilds.** The Layers panel rebuilds the whole map's ground from the base (`geo.ApplyHeights`), which would undo ground edits made since. A water edit instead re-runs the Creeks and Lakes layers and patches the ground only where their output changed (`world.GroundPatch`, as the 2026-10-13 lake migration did), then redoes the material map, the lake grid and its snow (`setUpLakes`, `sim.ApplyLakes`), and removes trees left standing in water.
- **The tool.** A Water tool in the editor, on the run tool's pattern (`scene/run_tool.go`): water lines drawn over the terrain while it's on; click a creek or lake to select it and show its node handles; drag a node; click the line to insert one; right-click a node to delete it; Delete removes the selected creek or lake.

## Steps

In order. Each step builds with `go build` and `go vet`, and is checked on Boreal and Kirkwood, on screen and against the lidar.

1. Done: **The Water layer.** `World.Water` (`WaterCreek`: a line from the source down; `WaterLake`: rings, name, kind, area), saved. The Water layer fits it the first time it runs (`geo.FitWater`), from the ground it's given (`geo.SeedCreeks`: trace, refine, join ends within 8 m of another creek's line, simplify to 1 m; `geo.SeedLakes`: `WaterMask` traced into rings round its samples, simplified to 1.5 m), then the lakes on the ground with those creeks carved), and the Creeks and Lakes layers build what's stored (`WaterCreekStreams`/`CreekLines`, `LevelLakes`/`ShapeMask`); a save whose Water layer hasn't run is fitted from its drawn ground (`scene.fittedWater`). The Water row refits on request. On Boreal: 72 creeks and 5 lakes (Crater Lake 16 points); creek ends near another creek's line but not on it, 44 before joining, 0 after.
2. Done: **Local water rebuild** (`scene.rebuildWater`). The Creeks and Lakes layers rerun with the old water and the new (`LayerStack.Invalidate`), and the ground is patched in 64-cell blocks where they differ, so other ground edits stay; then the material map and lake grid (each lake keeping its ice), lake snow, snow onto ground that left a lake, and trees in water. Boreal 0.12 s, Kirkwood 0.7 s, after the stack's first run (2.2 s and 8.5 s), which the tool does in the background when it opens.
3. Done: **The Water tool** (editor toolbar, `scene/editor_water.go`, on the Ski Area tool's pattern). Creeks drawn blue and lakes cyan over the ground; click one to select it (yellow, its points as handles); drag a point, drag the dot between two to add one, right-click a point to remove it, Delete removes the creek or lake, Esc lets go; a creek's end dragged near another creek snaps onto it (a point within a handle's reach, else its curve within 5 m), and a mouth dropped on a source (or a source on a mouth) makes the two one creek; the ground and water rebuild on release. Waits for the stack's first run, and for any terrain-layer rebuild.
4. **Notes and the shipped scenarios** (the user is fixing Boreal's and Kirkwood's water by hand, 2026-10-13). [[Scenario Editor]] and [[Terrain Import]] cards updated; Boreal and Kirkwood re-seeded and any visible water problems fixed by hand with the tool.

## Open questions

- Undo: the editor has none for other tools. Is one level of undo for a water edit worth it, given a rebuild per edit?
- A lake's level: from the ground inside its outline (as now), or a number the editor can set?

Every creek in the water now flows and is carved its whole length, catchment only sizing its channel (before, a creek dragged where less than 0.5 km² drained through it was neither carved nor shown); the fit keeps only the flowing part of OpenStreetMap's lines (Boreal: 32 creeks, not 72). Lakes cut their shore down to a 20° slope within 8 m of the water (`LevelLakes`), so a lake moved onto a slope sits in a hollow, not behind a step. Moving water puts the ground back where it was lowered for the old place (the rebuild compares the layers with the old water and the new): on a fresh Boreal, a creek point moved 40 m was carved 1.8 m at its new place and its old place restored exactly; a lake edge moved 20 m up a slope was cut in 4.2 m, its new shore sloped. Maps whose ground was carved before the Water layer existed keep any old channel the fit from their ground doesn't account for; a re-import starts clean.
- 2026-10-13: Winter cover per creek and lake (the user): `WaterCover` Auto, Open or Covered, set from a panel with the creek or lake selected in the Water tool, saved on the water and the lake (`Lake.Cover`). Auto is the season's behaviour (a creek open by its size with snow bridges, a lake freezing from its shallows). An Open lake never has ice, so it takes no snow; a Covered lake always has at least 0.3 m of ice, counts as frozen from the season's start (`LakeSeason.FrozeOn`), and gets the snow of the ground round its shore when switched (`snowOntoCoveredLakes`). An Open creek is open water its whole length, a Covered one snow-bridged; creeks change the material map only, since their snow is per 5 m corner.
- 2026-10-13: Creeks run along a curve through their points (the user): `WaterCreek.Curve`, a centripetal Catmull–Rom spline sampled every metre, which passes through each point without overshooting. The channel's carving, the creek's water and snow bridges, the water sheet, and the Water tool's drawing, picking and add-a-point dots all follow it; the points stay what's dragged. The fit eases the refined line's side-to-side wobble before thinning it (`easeLine`), so a fitted creek curves smoothly.
- 2026-10-13: Saw-toothed creek banks (the user). Cause: deep snow (~0.85 m) was cut off sample by sample along the 1.25 m material lattice's staircase at the water's edge, so the edge showed each sample as a tooth; the material map itself was clean. Now the terrain shaders measure open water as a 3×3 average of bilinear samples, eased (tese for the snow's depth, frag for the colour, with a little noise), and snow depth blends bilinearly between 5 m corners in the mesh and in `VisualElevationAt`, so a bank slopes into the water.
- 2026-10-13: Joining creeks and deeper channels (the user). Dragging a creek's first or last point near another creek snaps it on, ringed green while snapped: onto a point within a handle's reach, else onto the curve within 5 m. On release a mouth on another's source, or a source on another's mouth, merges them into one creek (the dragged one's name and cover, else the other's name); anywhere else it's a junction. Channels are deeper (`CreekShape` depth 0.6 + 0.5·√km², was 0.4 + 0.4·√km²) and the creek's water 0.35 m over its bed (was 0.25). Maps carved before keep their old depth until the Layers panel rebuilds the ground, or where a water edit re-carves.
