# Changelog

- 2026-10-15: Saving is quicker: Kirkwood 20 s → 1.1 s, Boreal 4 s → 0.2 s. The 1 m groom map (160 MB on Kirkwood, almost all empty) saves only its groomed pixels, and the save compresses at gzip's fastest level (files about 10% bigger). Older saves rebuild grooming from the cells. The editor's Glade tool matches play's: a Thin slider beside Radius, the trees it will take shown in red, and each click or cell dragged into thinning once (it took 40% every frame held). Glading no longer redraws the whole forest for each cell (every one of Kirkwood's 300,000 trees, every frame in the editor): felled trees drop out of the drawn forest alone, with one full redraw when the mouse is released. Building, lift, road, rail, path and terrain-brush edits no longer lock the game on big maps: on Kirkwood a building drop was about a second of whole-map work. The terrain mesh now rebuilds and re-uploads only the chunks round changed ground (4 ms and under 1 MB against 180 ms and 39 MB; identical to a full rebuild), and only the changed part of the detail texture; tree wells restamp only cells whose trees changed (270 ms → 3 ms per graded pad); the forest keeps its trees' draw data between rebuilds (150 → 11 ms). Whole-map snow changes (Auto snow, Clear Snow) still rebuild everything. Skier tracks, tree wells and groom edges draw on Kirkwood again: its surface texture (17,880 px across at 25 cm a pixel) was over the GPU's 16,384 px limit and failed to allocate without a word, so the shader read nothing; the texture is now cut into 2,048 px tiles, the layers of one array texture, each with a one-pixel apron from its neighbours so no seam shows. Avalanches ([[Avalanche Control]]): debris is its own thing on a cell (saved), not a snow kind the next day's weather wiped, and is drawn as white lumpy debris with soft shadowed hollows and 3D snow blocks where it's deep (it was a flat warm grey); new snow buries it, a thaw slumps it, a cat clears it. Slides run as a stream down their gully, pick up snow on the way (a slab is now most of the top layer, not half), keep going the way they were heading on flat ground and pile up in a runout lobe that then slumps into a cone, instead of spreading over hectares in a film a few millimetres deep. A running slide raises a billowing powder cloud; a mortar round flashes and smokes at the patrol room, trails smoke on its arc and lands in a blast of snow; a ski cutter traverses the start zone and the slope cracks round them. A slide across the runs, or anyone caught, pauses the game with a window to go to it (Pause for avalanches in Settings to turn off); control work says so in a toast. The grey patch in the Kirkwood save was bare cliff rock, not debris. Avalanche control works only the paths at or over the player's limit (it shelled everything Moderate or worse every dawn, so the limit never saved money), can be paused from the patrol popup, and shows the day's spend; slide paths' loads are saved, so continuing a game no longer resets the danger nearly to safe. [[Skier Collisions]] done and off Priority (the readable step and tuning by eye left over in its note); Priority empty. Snowpack fixed against real snow-station records ([[Snow]], [[Weather]]): snow and rain by altitude, more precipitation higher up, and a winter pack that barely melts, so a resort holds a base from December (Carson Pass now within about 10% of its station all season; Palisades Tahoe within 3–15% from January). OpenStreetMap railways, stations and named peaks: on the import map and the editor's overlay, and peaks become map labels shown in play with their height, which the editor's new Labels tool adds, moves, renames and deletes ([[Terrain Import]], [[Scenario Editor]]). Skiing physics: edges no longer brake a traverse (only a skid costs speed) and air drag is a real skier's, so skiing (the player's and the guests') is faster and a turn no longer stops you on a gentle run; gravity follows the true fall line on gentle ground too, so you no longer glide on forever pointed uphill; groomed snow glides faster than ungroomed, fresh powder drags by its depth, and skis bite near a standstill so a stop stays a stop; guests' speed checks, beginners' ploughing and collision speeds retuned to keep falls and ratings where they were ([[Skiing]]). Performance with big crowds ([[Crowd Scale]]): a frame steps the sim for at most 10 ms, so a busy day at fast or fastest runs slower than the button instead of dropping to 3 fps; route searches round the trees expand each cell once (they reopened cells about 11 times, and one search could hold a step up 80 ms), smoothing normal speed and cutting a day's sim time 15–20%. A popup's close button over the top or bottom bar works (the bars took the click; windows now take clicks first). Lift lines' ends cached, so guests heading for a crowd no longer rescan it every step: the peak hour 25–35% faster, play unchanged ([[Crowd Scale]]). [[Saved Games]] planned and built: a game is named after its scenario ("Boreal", "Boreal 2") and renamable, saves itself on starting, each midnight and on leaving, and is written safely with a backup; the games list continues, renames, copies and deletes; the scenario picker offers Continue or New game.

Track drawn in its style (steam: wooden sleepers; electric: concrete and masts; high-speed: slab track, gantries), platforms too; railways in the scenario editor; stations dragged along the track, R to turn the platform round. Clicking off the map finishes track at its edge. [[Editor Parity]]: the editor has every lift type and a lift popup, ski racks and ropes, and a test run (pause, play, fast) that runs the lifts and trains empty. Stations go on the mouse's side of the track; track, roads and paths being laid show their course over the ground. Tunnel mouths meet the hill (no longer dug out behind the portal), cuts are steeper rock cuts, and trees go from cut and filled ground. Stations level a pad for their platform and house. Track being laid shows red only where a click would finish it and it couldn't be built. Tunnel mouths redone for rail, road and path: no bowl round the portal, the hill trimmed back from its top, the terrain cut out where it would slope through the opening, roads and paths stopping at the face. The editor runs its lifts and trains from the start; pause holds them still to look at. Terrain drawing back to full speed (the tunnel cut-outs had slowed the whole terrain; now only the chunks with a mouth use them). Trains come out of tunnels instead of popping out past the portal; portals have no sill across the opening. Steam engines puff steam from their chimneys, trailing back over the train. The mouse's point on the ground no longer jumps (from [[Bugs]], found in Golf Mogul): `screenToWorld` marches the mouse's ray toward the ground as drawn, in steps that shrink near it, and bisects 16 times; on Boreal and Kirkwood the point moved up to 2 m between neighbouring pixels and sat up to 1.9 m off the ground, now a pixel's width and on it. The editor's fast is 8×. Performance: each frame's time is rounded to whole display refreshes so panning moves evenly (the loop's clock jitters 4–13 ms a frame at 120 Hz and the camera juddered with it; a 60 fps cap was tried and dropped: macOS ignores a swap interval of 2, and holding 60 by timer on a 120 Hz screen made the menu scene jumpier); the terrain shader does its lake-ice noise only near ice and its broad snow patchiness and drifts a vertex rather than a pixel (15–20% off a frame, the same look); GPU timing tools ([[Debug Tools]]).
